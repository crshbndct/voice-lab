Practice phrase

A common feminine speaking range is about 165–255 Hz. 145–175 Hz reads as androgynous. Aim to sit comfortably in range, not at the top of it.

What the numbers mean

Pitch

How high or low your voice is. Get into range, then stop chasing it. A strained high pitch sounds less feminine, not more.

The summary's “melody” number is how much your pitch moves around. More movement sounds more expressive.

Resonance

How big or small your vocal tract sounds, measured from the formants (F2 and F3). This matters more than pitch for being read as a woman.

Raise it with a higher larynx and a smaller mouth space: the “big dog, small dog” panting drill, or sliding from “mmm” into speech.

Vocal weight

The gap between your first two harmonics (H1–H2). Thick, buzzy vocal folds give a low number and a lighter fold contact gives a higher one.

Lighter is the goal, but don't go breathy. If loudness drops a lot when weight goes up, you're adding air instead of thinning the folds.

Loudness

Relative to your phone's mic, so only compare it with your own sessions. Use it to check you aren't getting quieter to sound feminine.

All numbers are rough estimates. Your ears are the final judge, so listen back to every session.

Running it live

Browsers only allow the mic on a secure web address. To use live mode on your iPhone, put this same page file online:

  1. Easiest: go to app.netlify.com/drop on your computer and drag the HTML file in. You get an https link you can open in Safari and add to your Home Screen.
  2. Or put it in a GitHub repo and turn on GitHub Pages.
  3. On your PC only: run python3 -m http.server in the file's folder and open localhost:8000. Localhost counts as secure.
